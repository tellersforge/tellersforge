# 2. The Golden Rule and the Check

*Set: Core*

> ### The Golden Rule
>
> High numbers are always good. You always roll equal to or under the number on your sheet.  
> A natural 1 always succeeds. A natural 20 always fails.

### The Basic check, unchanged

This is how Basic rolls every skill and power, and it still works at a Core table:

> Value = the skill's bold attribute + Lvl/Mod, lowered by 4 if the skill lists any other attributes. Roll one d20. Your value or lower succeeds.

The −4 stands in for the dice that Basic does not roll. A table that likes the speed of one die may keep it for every check (Table Settings, chapter 14); the odds are close to the full check across the ordinary range of attributes, a little kind to low scores and a little hard on high ones.

### The Core check: one die per attribute

Each skill lists the attributes it uses, for example Blacksmithing **STR**/DEX/END. In Core you roll **one d20 for each attribute listed**, and each die must come up equal to or under its attribute plus your Lvl/Mod and anything else that applies. The −4 is gone; the dice do its work.

Every die must succeed. If any die fails, the check fails. So a skill with more dice is harder. The lexicon groups skills by number of dice, which also sorts them from easiest to hardest.

**Example.** Mira has STR 12, DEX 14, END 10 and Blacksmithing +3. In Basic her Blacksmithing value was 12 + 3 − 4 = 11, one die. In Core she rolls three dice against 15, 17 and 13, and all three must pass.

### The fate die

The first attribute listed, printed in bold, is the one the skill depends on most. Roll the fate die, the odd-coloured one, for that attribute, and the other dice for the rest. The fate die is where luck lives:

- **A natural 1 on the fate die:** the whole check succeeds, whatever the other dice say.
- **A natural 20 on the fate die:** the whole check fails, whatever the other dice say.
- Anything from 2 to 19: an ordinary die.

That is all it does. However skilled or clumsy you are, there is a one-in-twenty chance of a surprise either way. In the Basic check, the one die you roll is the fate die.

### Adding it up

For each die, start with the attribute, then add or subtract everything that applies: your Lvl/Mod, the situation, tools, magic items. There is no upper limit. The lowest a target can go is 1.

**Example.** INT 19. Lvl/Mod +1. Rushed, −3. A magic lens, +6. Total 23. The room goes dark, −10. Final target 13. The player rolls 11: that die succeeds.

A non-fate die with a target of 20 or more cannot fail. Only the fate die can still bring the whole check down, on a natural 20.

### Easier and harder

The Game Master may add to your targets when the task is easy, or take away when it is hard. The same change applies to every die of the check.

| The task is                        | Change    |
|------------------------------------|-----------|
| Easy (climbing a knotted rope)     | +3        |
| Normal                             | no change |
| Hard (climbing a wet wall)         | −3        |
| Very hard (a wet wall in the dark) | −6        |
| Nearly impossible                  | −10       |

### When fate breaks the odds: the Luck roll

Sometimes the fate die says the impossible: a natural 1 on something the character could not do, a natural 20 on something they could not get wrong. Then roll for **Luck**: one d20 against the character's Luck attribute. At or under, fortune is with you, and the impossible result stands as a stroke of luck. Over, it turns against you.

| Fate die   | The numbers alone would have… | Luck roll  | Call it                                      |
|------------|-------------------------------|------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Natural 1  | passed                        | not needed | Spectacular success: skill at its best       |
| Natural 1  | failed                        | passed     | Good luck: you couldn't succeed, but you did |
| Natural 1  | failed                        | failed     | A plain failure after all                    |
| Natural 20 | failed                        | not needed | Spectacular failure: skill at its worst      |
| Natural 20 | passed                        | passed     | Luck waves the failure off: a plain success  |
| Natural 20 | passed                        | failed     | Bad luck: you couldn't fail, but you did     |

Luck balances out. Good luck succeeds but costs something: the thief opens the vault and is knocked out before she can take anything. Bad luck fails but may turn out strangely: thrown across the room by the trap, the character lands on the real treasure.

### Luck in play

Luck is an attribute like the other nine, rolled the same way: one d20, your Luck or lower succeeds. Beyond rescuing the fate die, the Game Master calls for it when chance alone decides and no skill covers it: the door happens to be unlocked, the rope happens to hold; for any gambling; for random encounters (rolled for the group: success means the trouble does not find you); and for a close miss (a check failed by only 1 or 2 may be saved by one Luck roll). What a success does is set by the rule or the table it serves, or by the Game Master where none applies; there is no one fixed effect and no limit on how often Luck may be rolled, because luck does not keep count.

### How badly? (deeper step)

First step: pass or fail. Deeper step: count the failed dice. One miss means you nearly made it and got part of the way. Most dice missing means it went badly.

### Skill levels: Lvl/Mod and rerolls

> ### A skill's level is also the modifier for that skill
>
> And a power's level is also the modifier for that power. A skill at level +3 adds +3 to every die it rolls; a power at level +2 adds +2. In play the skill's level and the skill's modifier are one number, and the sheet's column is called Lvl/Mod for that reason. Core adds a second box, Rerolls, for growth beyond +5; it does not change what a skill's level is.

Each skill on the Core sheet has two pencil boxes: **Lvl/Mod** and **Rerolls**.

- **Lvl/Mod** is added to every die in the check. It grows from +1 to +5. Basic stops here.
- **Rerolls** start once the Lvl/Mod reaches +5. Each reroll lets you roll one failed die again in that check. You may reroll the same die more than once. Rerolls refresh every check; unused ones do not carry over.
- The most rerolls a skill can have equals its number of dice. A 1-die skill tops out at 1 reroll; a 3-dice skill at 3.
- A natural 20 on the fate die's first roll cannot be rerolled. On a reroll, 1 and 20 are ordinary numbers.
- When both boxes are full, the skill is **mastered**. It cannot grow further.

The sheet shows each skill group's maximum, for example MAX \| +5 \| 3 \| for 3-dice skills. In the Basic check, a reroll is a reroll of the one die.

### Skill tiers

Names for how good someone is. The boxes are the rule; the names are for flavour.

| Tier      | Level        | On the sheet                                                                              |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Unknown   | −6           | −6 on every die. Something from outside the character's world, like computers to a Roman. |
| Untrained | 0            | Lvl/Mod 0                                                                                 |
| Novice    | 1–2          | Lvl/Mod +1 to +2                                                                          |
| Practised | 3–5          | Lvl/Mod +3 to +5                                                                          |
| Adept     | first reroll | +5 and 1 reroll                                                                           |
| Expert    | between      | +5 and more rerolls, not yet full                                                         |
| Master    | full         | +5 and all rerolls                                                                        |

A skill can also sit at a negative level (−1 to −6): write the penalty in the Lvl/Mod box. A −1 is a neglected skill. Unknown (−6) is locked: it cannot be raised with points or BONUS rolls. Only long immersion lifts it: when the Game Master judges, during play, that the idea is no longer foreign to the character, it moves to −1. From −1 it grows the normal way. Any other negative level is raised the normal way, one level at a time.

### Attribute checks

When no skill fits, roll a plain attribute: one d20, the attribute or lower. Forcing a door is Strength; noticing a sound is Perception; holding your nerve is Courage. The Game Master may ask for two attributes together, one die each, both must pass: intuition is Wisdom and Perception.
