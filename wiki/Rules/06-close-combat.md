# 6. Close Combat

*Set: Core*

Fighting is the same game at every layer of Teller's Forge. It runs on five factors, **Attack, Parry, Dodge, Armour** and **Range**, with Damage, Life and Initiative as their results. Combat uses one d20 to hit and one d20 to defend, never the multi-dice skill check, so that fights stay quick. All the arithmetic is done once, on the sheet, before play. This chapter first restates Basic's combat unchanged, then adds what Core brings.

### The Basic combat values, unchanged

| Value         | How to work it out                                                      |
|---------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Max Life      | Endurance + your profession's extra Life. Current Life starts the same. |
| Melee Attack  | Add Strength, Dexterity and Courage, divide by 3, round down.           |
| Ranged Attack | Add Perception, Dexterity and Courage, divide by 3, round down.         |
| Parry         | Melee Attack − 3. With a shield, Melee Attack − 1.                      |
| Dodge         | Dexterity − 3, minus your armour's Dodge penalty.                       |
| Initiative    | Your Courage. Highest acts first.                                       |
| Armour total  | The value of what you wear. Layers add together.                        |

Basic used one generic weapon group, "the weapons of your setting", so every melee weapon shared one Attack and every ranged weapon another. Those two values stay valid in Core for any weapon the character has no group for.

### Core adds: weapon groups

In Core, fighting skill is divided by the kind of weapon: Swords, Axes and Maces, Spears and Polearms, Staves, Daggers, Brawling, Bows, Crossbows, Slings and Thrown, Handguns, Long Guns, Energy Weapons and more (Gear / Core lists them, each with its three attributes and the era it needs). A weapon group is written on the sheet like a skill, with a Lvl/Mod, and it grows like a skill: points at Level Up, BONUS rolls, rerolls after +5. It is not a skill in the lexicon sense, because every character already fights; the group is how well they fight with that kind of weapon.

- **Base** = the average of the weapon group's three attributes, rounded down. For a Basic character's generic group, the three are Strength, Dexterity and Courage (melee) or Perception, Dexterity and Courage (ranged), exactly as above.
- **Attack** = Base + your Lvl/Mod in that group (−6 Unknown, 0 untrained, +1 to +5).
- **Parry** = Attack − 3, for close-combat weapons only. Write — for bows, guns and thrown weapons. With a shield, add its bonus (chapter 8).
- **Damage** = the weapon's damage, plus the bonus from the attribute named on the weapon's line (Basic's table: that attribute 6–7 gives −1; 8–13 none; 14–15 +1; 16 or more +2), plus your **skill damage bonus**: −1 below level 0, +1 at level 6, +2 at 7, +3 at 8. A fighter first learns to hit, then learns to hurt. Natural attacks (claws, bite) also add the size's force (Characters / Core).
- **Dodge** = Dexterity − 3 + your size's Dodge − your armour's Dodge penalty. Size Dodge: tiny +2, small +1, medium 0, large −1, huge −2, gigantic −4.
- **Life** = Endurance + profession Life bonus + your size's Life + 2 × any Toughness power level.
- **Initiative** = Courage.

**Example.** Borin the dwarf Fighter had Melee Attack 12 in Basic from STR 13, DEX 12, COU 13. In Core he takes the Axes and Maces group at +3 (his Fighter's main weapon). Its attributes are STR, DEX, COU, the same three, so Base 12, Attack 15, Parry 12. His axe does 1d6+1, no attribute bonus at STR 13, and no skill bonus until level 6.

### Who goes first

Highest Courage acts first, no roll. That is Basic's rule and it stands. Core adds, for ties or when it matters: everyone rolls a d20 under Courage. Successes beat failures; between successes, the higher roll wins.

### A round, unchanged

1.  The attacker rolls a d20 equal to or under Attack. Miss: nothing happens.
2.  The defender chooses: Parry (roll under Parry) or Dodge (roll under Dodge). Success: no hit.
3.  Hit: roll the weapon's damage and add the bonus. Take the defender's Armour total off it. What is left comes off Current Life. If armour takes it all, the hit does nothing. Armour is never used up.

Exactly equal succeeds. In a contest, a tie goes to the defender.

### Parrying more than one attack

Each weapon or shield you hold can parry once per round, each against a different attack. Sword and shield against two attackers: parry one with each. Against a single attacker there is still only one attack to parry. Being outnumbered is dangerous.

### Core adds: aiming at weak spots

- **Weak point** (armpit, joint, knee): −4 to Attack; armour counts half, rounded down.
- **Vital gap** (eye slit, throat): −8 to Attack; armour is ignored.

A weak fighter against heavy armour wins by aiming, not by hitting harder. A targeted hit that lands may also add a story effect, the Game Master's call: a cut tendon, a lost grip.

### Core adds: natural 1s and 20s in combat

- **Natural 1 on Attack:** a lucky hit that only a natural 1 on Parry or Dodge can stop. What a critical hit does is a table setting. Recommended ★: roll the weapon's damage dice twice, add the fixed number once, then armour as usual.
- **Natural 1 on Parry:** a lucky parry, and a free attack right away.
- **Natural 20 on Attack:** a miss. A table may add a **fumble**: roll Attack again; a second failure is a mishap: drop the weapon, lose your next parry, hit yourself or an ally, or go one step down the fatigue ladder. Recommended ★: one mishap, no confirmation roll. A table may use no penalty, or a comic mishap, instead.

### Core adds: the stalled fight

After two rounds with no damage done on either side, the Game Master calls a contest: Strength early in a fight, Endurance later. Both roll a d20 under the attribute; the loser loses their next parry or is pushed back a step. Fights should not stall for ever.

### Core adds: morale

Most fights end with one side fleeing or giving up. The Game Master decides when; roll Courage only if it is really unclear. For a group of opponents, roll once for the leader. The first death on their side, their leader falling, or half of them down are the usual moments to ask.

### Core adds: limb damage (deeper step)

When a hit clearly lands on a limb, name it and agree a −1 or −2 to whatever that limb does until it is treated. No chart.

### Core adds: size in a fight (deeper step)

A creature two sizes larger than you cannot be parried with a weapon; you can only Dodge. A creature two sizes smaller is hard to hit with a big weapon: −2 to Attack. Great bodies strike more often: huge and gigantic creatures get two attacks a round against medium or smaller targets. Sizes and their numbers are in Characters / Core and the Bestiary.
