# 11. Equipment and Carrying

*Set: Core*

Basic gave every character a kit and no money. Core adds money, prices, carrying and the rest; Gear / Core lists every item with its weight, era and price.

### Carrying

Gear is tracked by weight. Most of the time nobody looks. When someone tries something silly, it settles the argument.

- **Strength sets how much you can carry** without effort: Strength × 2 kilograms (a Strength 12 character carries 24 kg: a travel pack, a weapon, light armour).
- You can carry up to twice that, but it is effort: **Endurance sets how many hours** you can keep it up, one hour per point.
- Past that time, Strength and Endurance both start down the fatigue ladder. Rest and food clear it.
- On long marches, move one stage per day instead of per scene. Bad terrain or weather: the Game Master lowers Endurance for the purpose, which shortens the time.

### Money and prices

Coins: 1 gold (G) = 10 silver (S) = 100 copper (C). 1 silver is an ordinary worker's pay for one day, in any era. In eras without coins, prices are barter value. In modern and future worlds, call the coins dollars, credits or whatever the world uses; the numbers stay the same.

- Prices in Gear / Core are for the era the item first appears in.
- **Factory-made:** from the steam era on, anything from an earlier era costs one tenth of its price (at least 1 C).
- **Selling second-hand:** half price.
- **Bartering** can move a price up to a quarter either way. The Game Master decides.

Starting wealth comes from social standing (Characters / Core). Your profession's kit is free, as in Basic; your standing's extras are free too; starting money buys the rest. If your size is bigger or smaller than medium, armour and clothing must be made to fit, and cost more: large ×2, huge ×4, gigantic ×8.

### Tools and the lack of them

Many skills need tools: picks for a lock, a kit for a wound, instruments for a survey. Without them the Game Master decides between −3 (improvised) and impossible. The right tools give no bonus; good tools (Gear / Core marks them) give +1.

### Your own gear

An item you made or earned yourself gives you +1 with it: the smith's own axe, a pilot's own ship, a sniper's own rifle. It works for no one else, magic or not.

### Wear and tear (deeper step)

Rarely used; suits survival games and long campaigns. If used, check gear only at natural pauses (end of session, making camp), and only items that saw hard use. Nothing wears out just from time.

### Vehicles and mounts

- **Driving:** Basic's Vehicles skill drives anything; Core splits it by kind (Driving, Driving Teams, Sailing, Aircraft Piloting, Spacecraft Piloting). The vehicle's Handling is added to the check in chases and tricky moments.
- **Chases:** a chase is a series of opposed driving checks, one per round. The winner gains a step. Three steps ahead ends the chase: the one in front escapes, or the one behind catches them.
- **Attacking a vehicle:** its Armour subtracts from damage like worn armour; the damage left comes off its Structure. At half Structure, its Handling drops by 2 and its top speed halves. At 0 it stops working. If it crashes, the people inside take the overflow, the Game Master's call.
- Vehicle weapons use the Vehicle Gunnery weapon group.
- Mounts are animals: use their Bestiary entry for fighting, and Animal Handling (Core: Riding, or Exotic Beasts) for tricky moments.

### Survival and strange places

- Basic's Survival skill covers every land. Core splits it by terrain: Forest, Jungle and Swamp, Desert, Arctic, Mountain, Plains and Steppe, Coast and Sea, Caves and Underground, Ruins and Wasteland. In a terrain you do not know, use your closest terrain skill at −3; otherwise you are untrained. A land from outside your world is Unknown (−6).
- Low or zero gravity (floating islands, space, falling fights): untrained, −3 to physical actions and close-combat Attack.
- High gravity (heavy worlds, infernal realms): untrained, −3 to physical actions and you start at Winded.
- Novice or better in the matching gravity skill: no penalty.
