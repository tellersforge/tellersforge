# 5. Flaws

*Set: Core*

Every character has nine flaws, one tied to each attribute except Luck, and may carry a tenth by choice. Basic gives every character the same nine, the **standard flaws**, and that is the first step. Core adds the choice: any standard flaw may be swapped for one from the catalogue in Characters / Core.

### The standard flaws, unchanged

| Attribute    | Its flaw       | What it is                                                           |
|--------------|----------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Strength     | Temper         | losing it; lashing out                                               |
| Dexterity    | Impatience     | acting before the moment is right                                    |
| Agility      | Claustrophobia | fear of confined spaces: tunnels, cells, crowded holds               |
| Endurance    | Indulgence     | food, drink, comfort, more than is good for you                      |
| Intelligence | Curiosity      | needing to know; opening the wrong door                              |
| Wisdom       | Recklessness   | ignoring the odds                                                    |
| Perception   | Superstition   | reading meaning into omens and signs                                 |
| Charisma     | Greed          | wanting more: gold, food, praise                                     |
| Courage      | Fear           | a particular fear, named by the player: heights, the dark, the dead… |

The standard flaws are broad on purpose, and two can overlap: Greed covers gold-lust and gluttony, and so does Indulgence. The Game Master decides which applies, and may use either.

### Scoring a standard flaw, unchanged

Roll 1d6 and add 1 for each (2 to 7), once, when the character is made. If you like, move points between flaws. Low flaw scores are good.

### Chosen flaws

In Core you may replace any standard flaw with a chosen flaw from the catalogue: fears (heights, the dark, the dead, crowds…), social failings (braggart, drunkard, prideful, kleptomaniac…), and the drawbacks of hard worlds (a lamed leg, a weak stomach, no head for machines…). Each fear is its own flaw. The catalogue's Opposes column names the attribute the flaw sits under; the chosen flaw takes that attribute's slot. Choose carefully. A flaw can hurt you badly, or it can make the whole table laugh. Fear of Heights is harmless in a swamp and deadly on a castle wall.

A chosen flaw is scored once, at creation: roll the dice shown on its entry in the catalogue (1d4+5 for the mild ones, 1d4+3 for the troublesome, 1d4+1 for the dangerous) and write the result as its score. Low is good: a dangerous flaw starts low and fires rarely; a mild one starts high and comes up often.

Keep most of your flaws mild and no more than two dangerous. Fun flaws should come up often; flaws that could ruin a character should be rare, and hit hard when they do.

### One rule for every flaw

When a flaw's trigger comes up, the Game Master asks for a flaw roll. Roll 1d20: if it is equal to or under the flaw's score, the flaw takes over, whether you like it or not, and you play it out. A frightened character freezes, flees or refuses to go on; a greedy one reaches for the gold; a reckless one charges.

**The Game Master may add a modifier to a flaw's score, up or down, by circumstance.** Claustrophobia is worse in a mine than in a cellar; Greed is worse in a treasury. A higher score fires more often. There is no separate "always on" kind of flaw: every flaw is a score, and the modifier is how circumstance bears on it.

> ### Two different rolls: don't mix them up
>
> - **At creation (once):** the scoring dice (1d6+1 for a standard flaw, or the dice on a chosen flaw's entry) give the flaw its score. You write it down and keep it.
> - **In play (every time the flaw's moment comes):** roll 1d20. At or under the score, the flaw fires. Higher, and the character keeps control this time.

**Example.** Tomas took Fear (heights); its entry says 1d4+3, and he rolled 6. The party must cross a rope bridge. He rolls 1d20 and gets 4: 4 is 6 or less, so the fear fires and Tomas refuses to cross. On a 9 he would have gone across, pale but steady. The bridge is also in a high wind; the Game Master could have added +2 to the score first.

### The tenth flaw, and flaws gained in play

Nine flaws are required. A player who wants a tenth may take one from the catalogue at creation, on the sheet's spare line, scored like any chosen flaw. Some flaws are not chosen; they happen to you. **Addicted** is the common one: a character who keeps using something the Game Master's world makes habit-forming gains Addicted as a flaw, written on the spare line. The Game Master sets its starting score by the substance and adds modifiers for cravings, withdrawal or fresh use. It is overcome like any flaw, by the flaw roll at Level Up. A character who chose the flaw **Easily Hooked** at creation starts any Addicted flaw with a bigger modifier.

Other flaws gained in play follow the same pattern: a lasting injury (chapter 10), a terror that will not fade, a debt that owns you. The Game Master names it, sets a score, and it is played like the rest.

### Overcome flaws

A flaw that reaches 0 is overcome. Leave it on your sheet at 0: it is a record of who your character used to be. It is not necessarily gone for good. When a character is overwhelmed by exactly what they once struggled with, the Game Master may bring it back into play.

### Rewards

A flaw that fires and is played out well earns 1 to 5 XP, the Game Master's call. A flaw played brilliantly, one that makes the whole table laugh or gasp, earns the top of that range. You may also simply choose to give in to a flaw without rolling; that counts. Game Masters: reward good play. That is the whole reason flaws exist.
