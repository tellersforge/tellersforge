# 3. Making a Character

*Set: Core*

Characters are made in the **Characters / Core** book, which holds the step-by-step walkthrough, the fifty-four Core species, the forty professions, the cultures and social standings, and the flaw catalogue. This chapter only says how it fits with this one.

> ### Already have a Basic character?
>
> Keep the sheet. Characters / Core opens with the question and sends you to its appendix, *Upgrading a Basic character*, which walks you through it: take the 4 back out of every skill and power value that had it, and roll the extra dice the Core way instead; choose an era, a culture and a social standing, and take what they give; split each broad Basic skill into the Core skills it covers, at the same level; swap any standard flaw for one from the catalogue if you like; and give your weapon its Core weapon group. Nothing else changes. Your Basic numbers were never wrong; Core just rolls more dice.

### The life story, in order

Core builds a character the way a life happens. Each step adds what that part of life taught them:

1.  **Species:** what you were born as, with its attribute changes, size and body talents.
2.  **Era:** the technology you grew up with. Everything after it is Unknown (−6) to you.
3.  **Culture and social standing:** where you grew up and your family's place there; the skills everyone raised that way picks up, your people's weapon, and what your upbringing neglected.
4.  **Profession:** what you became. Last, with the final word: four main skills +3 (Basic's six professions have the Mods Basic gave them), two supporting +1, two neglected −1, extra Life, kit.
5.  **Attributes:** roll 2d6+4 ten times, as in Basic, and apply the species changes. (Core also offers point-sharing: roll the ten and share the total out as you like.)
6.  **Flaws:** the nine standard flaws, scored 1d6+1 as in Basic, or swapped for chosen flaws scored by the dice on their entries (chapter 5).
7.  **Skills:** add the Mods from every step; work out each value (chapter 2).
8.  **Powers:** if your profession or species gives them (chapter 12).
9.  **Gear and money:** your kit, your standing's extras, and starting money to spend (chapter 11).
10. **Combat values:** Attack, Parry, Dodge, Armour, Life, Initiative, once (chapter 6).

Basic did steps 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 in its own eight steps and left out 2 and 3. Core adds those two and deepens the rest.

### What a Game Master tells the players first

- The world's era, or era range.
- Whether powers exist, and how characters get them.
- Which species fit the world (a suggestion, never a fence), and whether high-power species are allowed.
- The Table Settings (chapter 14).
