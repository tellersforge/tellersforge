# 12. Powers and Magic

*Set: Core*

Skip this chapter if your world has no magic, psychic powers or strange technology. Basic's Mage and its twelve powers stand unchanged here; Core adds the full way of checking a power, higher versions of powers, more powers, and more ways to come by them.

### Powers are skills

A power is written on the sheet like a skill and rolled like one. It lists its attributes, the one it depends on most in bold. In the Basic check you roll one die against the bold attribute plus Lvl/Mod, lowered by 4 if the power lists any other attributes. In the Core check you roll one d20 per attribute, every die must pass, fate die on the bold one. Powers level exactly like skills: Lvl/Mod to +5, then rerolls up to the number of dice; both full is mastery. Powers take one to five dice: small wonders one or two, solid powers three, major ones four, world-shaking ones five.

### The Magic Energy Pool, unchanged

Max Energy = Intelligence + Wisdom. Current Energy starts the same. Casting a power on your action: roll against its value. Success: it works, and you take its Cost off Current Energy. Failure: nothing happens, and you still lose half the cost, rounded down. You cannot cast a power if you do not have enough Energy left. A night's sleep gives back 1d6 Energy; a full day of quiet rest gives back all of it.

Core adds a table setting for worlds where power is paid for differently: **fatigue** instead of Energy (every cast is a step down the ladder of chapter 9, the cost in Energy telling you how many: 2 is none, 4 is one, 6 is two), or **uses per day** (a power may be cast a number of times a day equal to its Lvl/Mod + 1). The Energy pool is the standard ★.

### Levels and versions

A power's level is its Lvl/Mod: it makes the cast more reliable, nothing else. A +5 Fire Blast is not bigger than a +2 Fire Blast; it just works more often. If you want a bigger effect, you need a bigger power. Powers / Core gives many powers in **versions**: Fire Blast I, II, III, each a power of its own with its own cost and its own, wider and heavier effect, and each with its own line on the sheet.

- Basic's twelve powers are the version I of each.
- You cannot learn version II until you have **mastered** version I: Lvl/Mod +5 and all its rerolls full, the same rule as for skills. Version III waits on mastery of II.
- A new version starts at Lvl/Mod +0 and grows like any power.
- A mastered lower version stays on your sheet and is still cast at its own cost. A master of Fire Blast III still lights candles with Fire Blast I.

**Example.** Fire Blast I (cost 6): a ball of flame the size of a head, 2d6 to the target and half to everyone within 2 metres. Fire Blast II (cost 10): the size of a cart, 3d6 and half within 4 metres. Fire Blast III (cost 16): a roaring sphere, 4d6 and half within 8 metres, and stone cracks. The numbers are in Powers / Core; this is the shape.

### Who can have powers

Your world decides: talent, bloodline, training or technology. The Game Master says what is possible. A Mage starts with three powers at Lvl/Mod +2, as in Basic; a Core Spirit-talker or Priest starts the same way from their own lists. A species that gives a power gives it as part of the body: a dragon-blooded warrior breathes fire even in a world without magic, because "no magic in this world" means no one can learn new powers, not that bodies stop working.

**Learning a new power:** it must be taught, found, or granted, in the story. Once it is yours, it goes on the sheet at Lvl/Mod +0 and may be raised with Level Up points and BONUS rolls. Basic's shortcut stands: a Mage may take a new power with a Level Up point at character level 3 (a 2-Energy power), 4 (4-Energy) or 5 (6-Energy); higher costs and higher versions always need the story.

### Damaging powers in a fight

They work like attacks: the caster rolls to cast, the target may Dodge (a shield-holder may Parry instead, for half damage), and armour comes off the damage. Damaging powers add the damage bonus (chapter 6) from the power's bold attribute. Lightning goes through armour and shields. A power that says it cannot be Dodged (Sleep) cannot be.

### Costumes: the same power from many sources

The same power can come from a wizard's study, a god's gift, a psychic mind, a machine or a mutation. Powers / Core calls these costumes. Telekinesis, a psychic shove and a wizard's unseen hand are one power with one entry; the costume is how it looks and what the world calls it.

### Arcane and clerical

Arcane power is broad and flexible: a trained mind bending what already exists. Clerical power is narrow but deep: it carries a god's authority. The same power can be worked either way. Crossed working: if a caster's thoughts turn to a god during an arcane working (for example on a natural 1 or 20 of the fate die), the result may come out dead, twisted or weak, or, very rarely, both broad and deep.

### Skills that look like magic

Fortune-telling, folk remedies, spirit rites, alchemy and the like are ordinary skills anyone can learn. A con man can read cards convincingly. They are only truly magical when the character also has the matching power. Then the two together make a Minor Work (chapter 13).

### Unknowing magic

Some peoples work real magic without calling it magic. Elves in some worlds think of their healing as simple craft; it is in fact a power. Write it as a Minor Work: first the skill the character believes in, then the power they do not admit to. The player knows it is magic; the character does not. It still counts as magic for everything else: magic-sight can see it, wards can block it, and it can be dispelled.

### Cosmic powers

Powers that reshape the world, raise the dead for good, or stop time are listed in the Expansions, not here. A Game Master may allow one to a player character; by default they belong to the world's great powers and to the Bestiary.
