# Vehicles and mounts


Every vehicle and mount, era by era: what it costs, how fast it goes and how it handles. How they work in play is in [Rules, chapter 11](Rules/11-equipment-and-carrying.md) (Vehicles and mounts); their guns are the [Vehicle Gunnery](Weapon_Groups/vehicle-gunnery.md) weapons.

- **Vehicle**: what it is. All names are plain descriptions; a table can call it whatever its world calls it.
- **Skill**: the skill you roll to drive, ride, sail or fly it. On big ships the captain rolls Ship Command and the helmsman rolls Sailing & Seamanship.
- **Needs**: the earliest Era point it can exist in. "—" means any era (magic or wonders).
- **Size**: medium, large, huge or gigantic, the same size classes as creatures.
- **Speed**: two numbers in kilometres per hour. The first is its steady travel speed over a day; the second is its top speed in a chase. Spacecraft give plain words instead: how long it takes to cross between planets, or how many light-years its jump drive covers in a day.
- **Crew**: how many people it needs to work it. **Passengers**: how many more it can carry.
- **Cargo**: how much it can carry on top of crew and passengers, in kilograms (kg) or tonnes (t). "pulls" means what it can drag in a cart or train.
- **Handling**: add this to your driving skill check in chases and tricky moments. +2 very nimble, +1 nimble, 0 normal, −1 and −2 lumbering, −3 very clumsy.
- **Armour**: on the same scale as worn armour (leather 3, plate 7, rifle plate 14). A number such as "30 front / 20 sides" means the front is tougher than the sides and back.
- **Structure**: how much damage it takes before it stops working, like Life for a person (a person has about 15 to 25). For an animal, "animal: see Bestiary" gives the creature's Life from the Bestiary; the name in brackets is its Bestiary entry.
- **Weapons**: guns and launchers built in. Their numbers are in **Vehicle weapons** at the end. "—" means none.
- **Price**: the list price in the era it first appears (1 silver, S, is an ordinary worker's pay for a day in that era). "—" means it can't simply be bought; the note says why. Animals don't include saddle and tack (40 S); carts and coaches don't include the animals that pull them.
- **Notes**: what it's for, or a quirk. Public transport gives a **fare** you pay to ride instead of buying.
