# Surveying

*Set: Expansion*

*aka: ______________________________*

Before a road, a canal or a boundary dispute, someone must measure the land. The surveyor carries chains, rods and sighting instruments, and walks the ground, marking distances, angles and heights and writing everything down. From those figures come maps, property lines and the plans that engineers build on. Without them, walls go astray, roads miss their towns and neighbours quarrel for generations.

It is patient, exact work, often done in rain, mud and thornbushes. Surveyors are respected by builders, trusted by courts and sometimes suspected by landholders, who fear that a careful measurement will find their field smaller than they thought. A good one can tell by eye whether ground is flat.

## Game block

- **Dice:** 3 · **Attributes:** **INT**/PER/DEX · **Mastered at:** +5 | 3

- **What failure looks like:** The measurements are off and the plan is wrong. Spectacular failure: a road, wall or boundary is built in the wrong place, and the error is only discovered once it matters.
