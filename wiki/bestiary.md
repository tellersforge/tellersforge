# Bestiary


Game Master material, for after character creation. Two parts: **Part 1, Peoples and kin**: every Breed/Race from the Species booklet as a ready-to-run NPC, and **Part 2, Creatures**: animals, beasts and monsters nobody plays. Each entry is a truncated stat block, just enough to run it at the table; the full numbers for any people are in the Species booklet.

**Count.** Part 1: 1863 stat blocks covering 2010 Breed/Race entries (identical numbers within a species share one block, as in the Species booklet). Part 2: 696 creatures.

## How to read a stat block

Every number is worked out with the same rules the characters use, so nothing here is special to the Bestiary:

- **Attack** = the weapon group's three attributes added and divided by 3 (round down), plus the skill modifier (level 1 to 5 gives +1 to +5; levels 6 to 8 give rerolls instead).
- **Parry** = Attack − 3, only with a weapon or a body that blocks; "—" means it can only dodge. **Dodge** = DEX − 3 + size Dodge − armour Dodge penalty.
- **Damage** = the weapon's damage on the Basic scale (a knife 1d4, a standard weapon 1d6+1, a heavy two-hander 2d6), plus size force for natural attacks, plus the bonus from the weapon's attribute (6–7 gives −1, 8–13 none, 14–15 +1, 16 or more +2).
- **Armour** = the best layer, worn or natural; a second layer adds +1 (armour catalogue rule).
- **Life** = END + the role's or creature's bonus + size Life + 2 × Toughness.
- **Threat** = how many trained human soldiers (Attack 14, longsword 1d6+1, boiled leather 3, Life 17) it takes to beat it at least half the time, found by simulating the fight. **minor** = one soldier wins nine times in ten. A threat 4 creature is a fair fight for four soldiers or a strong party of adventurers. Threat counts bodies and weapons only: a **+** after it means the creature also has attacking powers (breath, blasts, mind control, fear, poison and the like), so it is more dangerous than the number says. Ranged attackers are also undercounted, since the test fight is at close quarters.
- **Morale** ends most fights (rules): the Game Master has a beaten side flee or surrender, rolling Courage only when unsure.

**Two draft rules introduced here (they apply to everyone, characters included):**

1. **Too big to parry.** An attack from a body two or more size classes bigger than yours can't be parried, only dodged. A soldier can parry an ogre's club, not a tyrant lizard's jaws; a tiny sprite can't parry a human's sword.
2. **Great bodies strike more often.** A huge creature makes at least 2 natural attacks a round (bite and stomp, trunk and tusks), a gigantic one at least 3. A creature's own entry can give more. Playable huge breeds keep the attacks their Species entry gives.

**Size effects** (Species rules page, rule 15): tiny, small, medium, large, huge and gigantic bodies differ in STR, AGI, force, Dodge, Life, carry, food, gear and spaces. Creature attributes in Part 2 are final numbers with size already included.

## Roles for peoples

A people's stat block in Part 1 is shown as a **guard or soldier**. For any other job, take the difference between that role and the guard in the table below (shown for an ordinary human) and apply it to the people's numbers. The table covers the most common NPC functions; the Game Master adds anything else from the Species booklet. Weapons are shown as pre-modern (longsword, longbow); in a later era, swap in that era's standard sidearm or rifle from the Weapon List and recalculate Damage.

| Role | Weapon (level) | Attack | Parry | Dodge | Damage | Armour | Life | Role skills |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guard or soldier | longsword (Swords 3) | 14 | 11 | 8 | longsword 1d6+1 | 1 (boiled leather, light) | 17 | Investigation +2, Intimidation +1 |
| Commoner | club or knife (Brawling 0) | 11 | 8 | 8 | club 1d6+1 or knife 1d4 | 0 (none) | 11 | a trade skill +3, Local Lore +2 |
| Veteran or elite | longsword (Swords 5) | 16 | 13 | 6 | longsword 1d6+1 | 2 (mail, medium) | 17 | Military Tactics +3, Investigation +3 |
| Hunter or archer | longbow (Bows 3) | 14 | 11 | 8 | longbow 1d6+1 | 1 (boiled leather, light) | 15 | Tracking +3, Stealth +2 |
| Thief or scout | dagger (Knives & Daggers 3) | 14 | 11 | 8 | dagger 1d4 | 0 (none) | 15 | Stealth +3, Lockpicking +3 |
| Priest or healer | staff (Staves 1) | 12 | 9 | 8 | staff 1d6 | 0 (none) | 13 | Religion & Theology +3, First Aid +3 |
| Adept or mage | staff (Staves 1) | 12 | 9 | 8 | staff 1d6 | 0 (none) | 13 | Arcana +3, Folk Magic +2 |
| Leader or noble | longsword (Swords 3) | 14 | 11 | 6 | longsword 1d6+1 | 2 (mail, medium) | 17 | Leadership +3, Etiquette +3 |
| Merchant or scholar | dagger (Knives & Daggers 0) | 11 | 8 | 8 | dagger 1d4 | 0 (none) | 11 | Haggling +3, Local Lore +3 |

**Example.** A people's guard block shows Attack 15, Parry 12, Life 19. As a merchant (Attack 3 lower and Life 6 lower than a guard in the table, no armour): Attack 12, Parry 9, Life 13, armour from its own body only, and Haggling +3, Local Lore +3 on top of its own best skills.

Tags after a name: **(NPC only)** a sentient people not open to players; **(beast)** a non-sentient kin, run as an animal (natural attacks at level 3, no gear, Life bonus 4); **(non-sentient)** a mindless body with hands, such as a war robot or drilled servitor, which still carries the role's weapon and armour; **(high-power)** playable only with the Game Master's approval; **(NPC-scale)** cosmic or godlike, far beyond any role here.

Every people's NPC row is on its species page, under each breed. The creatures are below.
