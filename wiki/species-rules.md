# Species rules


How species numbers work: three layers (family, species, breed or race), three operations (Set, Mod+, Mod−), and the rules that keep them consistent and balanced. The families are listed at the end; each family page lists its species.
The first of three layers. Family sets the defaults for every species and Breed/Race under it; species changes only what differs from its family; Breed/Race changes only what differs from its species. Anything not listed is 0.

## How the layers work

**Three operations.** Each layer can do one of three things to a skill or power:

- **Set:** replaces whatever came from the layer above with a fixed value.
- **Mod+:** adds to what came from above.
- **Mod−:** subtracts from what came from above.

**Order.** Start at 0 for everything. Apply Family, then Species, then Breed/Race. Inside each layer, Sets first, then Mods. A Set lower down wipes out everything above it for that one line; Mods keep stacking.

**Then, in this order:** Era block (a skill past the character's era is Foreign Concept, whatever the species gives), then archetypes (culture, station, profession), then the caps of chapter 3, step 7: above 8 counts as 8, nothing below Foreign Concept (−6).

**Example.** Canine family gives Tracking Mod+3. A scent-hound breed adds Mod+2: Tracking ends at +5. A lap-dog breed sets Tracking to 0: it ends at 0, ignoring the family's +3.

## Consistency rules

1. **Size of a Mod.** 1 = a slight edge, 2 = a clear edge, 3 = the defining trait. No single Mod goes above 3 at any layer.
2. **Standard Set values only:** Foreign Concept (−6) when the body simply can't do it (a centaur riding a horse, living stone swimming); 0 to wipe an inherited edge; +1, +2, +3 or +5 for built-in ability.
3. **Separate steps.** Attributes, skills, powers, Works and gear are separate steps in character creation, and each stands on its own. The game can be played with base attributes alone, so species attribute modifiers are their own step, set and balanced on their own, and never refer to skills. Attributes still drive every check as usual: a skill rolls against its attributes, so a high STR helps every STR skill. The separation is only about where species modifiers are written. A species' attribute modifiers go in the attribute step and do their work through the normal roll; the skill lines on this page add only what is special to the species at that skill on top of that, and never repeat an attribute bonus as a skill bonus. Gear capability never appears here either; what a species' body can wear or use is a gear-fitting matter.
4. **Powers.** No species has a power unless a layer Sets it. **Set** grants the power at that level. **Mod+ on a power is affinity:** it does nothing until the character gains the power some other way (training, a gift), then it adds. A Set on a lower layer replaces the affinity, like any Set. Affinity scores 0 in the balance count.
5. **Choice lines** (Humankind, Unknown humanoid, Hybrid) are ordinary Mod+ or Mod− lines where the player or Game Master picks the target. No fourth operation.
6. **Great and Minor Works need no entries.** A Work is a skill phase plus a power phase, so the species' skill and power values flow into it automatically.
7. **Weapon groups** (Brawling, Swords, Bows and so on) get their species lines in a separate pass, with the same three operations.
8. **Balance is judged per step, on the finished Breed/Race.** The family totals below are for information. The balance-checker (build list) checks each step on its own (attributes, then skills, then powers), then the whole character.
9. **Identical stats share one stat block, within the same parent only.** Breeds whose numbers all match *and* that belong to the same species share one set of stats. Species whose numbers all match *and* that belong to the same family share one set of stats. Families whose numbers all match share one set of stats. Nothing is ever grouped across species or across families: the point is to save room on the page, not to confuse players. "Numbers" means every game number (attributes, skills, powers, weapon groups, size class, movement); if even one differs, it is a separate stat block. What still differs within a shared block (size within the same size class, colour, weight, build, appearance, culture, homeland, customs) goes on each one's own lexicon entry, which points to the shared block. This mirrors the weapon stubs: one set of numbers, many names.
10. **Story-only details are lexicon only.** Lifespan, age, size within a size class, colour, weight, appearance, homeland, customs and history go on the lexicon entry as prose. They never become a skill or power line. **The one exception is age:** a long life lets a character span more than one era, so lifespan feeds the Era step (the character's native eras) and nothing else. A being who has lived through the bronze age and the age of sail can know both eras' skills without the Foreign Concept penalty. (Long life is therefore never a Longevity line; the Longevity power is only for characters who actively slow their own ageing.)
11. **Weapon groups (2026-09-25).** Weapon groups are skills and take the same three operations, but only for the body: extra arms, grasping tentacles, constricting coils, a centauroid's charge, a huge body's hurling strength. A people's weapon tradition (bows, axes) belongs to the Culture step.
12. **No hands, no held weapons.** A body without hands can't use held weapon groups. This follows from the body plan, so no lines are written; entries say "No hands: held weapons unusable." in their note.
13. **Natural attacks: the weapon sets the base, force sets the rest.** A permanent claw, bite, horn, tusk, hoof, tail, pincer, beak, stinger or slam is a natural attack, fought with Brawling and inherited like everything else (a lower layer can Set it to 0 to remove it). **The same natural weapon always has the same base damage, whoever carries it:** light **1d6** (beak, tongue, snake hair, swarm bites) · standard **1d6+1** (bite, claws, talons, tail, mandibles, fangs, stinger, tentacle lash, spines, slam, stone fist, kick) · strong **1d6+2** (hooves, pincers, blade arms, sickle claws, inner jaw, stomp) · heavy **1d6+3** (horns, antlers, tusks, smashing claws). **Force** is added on top: the heavier or stronger the creature, the harder it hits. Draft force by size: tiny −2, small −1, medium 0, large +2, huge +4 (colossal beings more, set with size class); unusually dense bodies (stone, metal) count one size heavier. Strength then applies as it does for weapons, with the Strength number set by the base (8 / 10 / 12 / 14): meet it +1, fall short −1. The force numbers are provisional until the size-class step. The **Natural Weapons power** is kept only for bodies that grow or extrude weapons at will.
14. **Attribute modifiers (own step, 2026-09-25).** Characters roll 2d6+4 seven times and share the total out; the species modifiers are added afterwards. They follow the same layers (family, species, breed) with Mod+ and Mod− of 1 to 3 per line. **Balance:** a playable Breed/Race's modifiers add up to between −1 and +3 (Humankind's free +1 counts), and no single attribute goes beyond ±4. NPC-only and NPC-scale beings are exempt (up to ±8). Raw strength, toughness, agility, intellect, judgement, presence and nerve live here, never in skill lines.
15. **Size class (draft).** Each Breed/Race has a size class, taken from its survey entry unless a layer Sets it (with the reason noted). **Size carries its own trade-off:** a bigger body is stronger but less agile, a smaller one weaker but more agile, so every size class shifts STR and AGI by the same amount in opposite directions. The shift costs nothing from the attribute budget in rule 14 and doesn't count toward the ±4 limit. **Where a breed's own line pushes the same way as the size shift, only the larger of the two counts** (a small, quick breed with AGI Mod+2 ends at +2, not +4). **The one exception is STR on large and bigger bodies:** there the breed's STR line means "strong even for its size", so it stacks. Draft effects, provisional until play-tested:

| Size | STR | AGI | Force (natural attacks, unarmed) | Dodge | Life | Carry limit | Food and water | Armour and clothing | Spaces |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tiny | -3 | +3 | -2 | +2 | -4 | x1/4 | x1/4 | made to order | fits almost anywhere |
| small | -2 | +2 | -1 | +1 | -2 | x1/2 | x1/2 | made to order | fits almost anywhere |
| medium | +0 | +0 | +0 | +0 | +0 | x1 | x1 | off the shelf | normal |
| large | +2 | -2 | +2 | -1 | +4 | x2 | x2 | made to order, x2 cost | stoops indoors; -2 in cramped places |
| huge | +4 | -4 | +4 | -2 | +8 | x4 | x4 | made to order, x4 cost | can't enter most buildings or boats |
| gigantic | +6 | -6 | +6 | -4 | +16 | x8 | x8 | made to order, x8 cost | outdoors only |
| varies | set per individual by the Game Master | | | | | | | | |

**Big bodies win up close, by design.** The balance checker shows large and huge breeds win most sword fights against an ordinary human, even after the STR/AGI trade. That is accepted: an ogre should win a brawl. Big bodies pay in everyday life instead, through the last three columns and their Dodge:

- **Food and water.** Rations, water and upkeep scale with size. A large traveller eats for two.
- **Armour and clothing.** Anything bigger or smaller than medium can't buy off the shelf. Big bodies pay more for it (large double, huge four times) and wait for it to be made; small and tiny bodies wait, but pay normal prices.
- **Spaces.** A large body stoops in human buildings and takes −2 on physical checks and Attack in cramped places (tunnels, ships' holds, low taverns). A huge body can't enter most buildings or boats at all.
- **Easy targets.** Lower Dodge, and Dodge is the only defence against arrows and bullets, so big bodies lose much of their edge in a ranged fight.

The balance report therefore doesn't flag a large or bigger breed just for winning sword fights; it still flags one that also dominates at range or carries heavy powers.

**Example.** A large breed with STR Mod+1 and no AGI line ends at STR +3, AGI −2. A small breed with AGI Mod+2 and STR Mod−1 ends at AGI +2 (the larger of its own +2 and the size's +2) and STR −2 (the larger of its own −1 and the size's −2).

16. **High-power breeds (2026-09-25).** A few playable breeds are strong because their sources are: superheroes, demigods, genies, angels, dragons and reality-benders. They keep their powers, and their note says **High-power (Game Master's approval)**. They suit super-powered or mythic campaigns; in an ordinary campaign the Game Master can refuse them, or use them as NPCs. The balance report lists them separately instead of flagging them.
17. **Natural armour stacks like worn armour (2026-09-25).** The armour catalogue's layering rule applies to bodies too: Armour Skin (value = its level) and Invulnerability (value = 2 × its level) are layers like any worn piece. Use the best layer on each zone; a second layer adds +1, never more. A beetle-folk with Armour Skin 3 wearing a padded jack (2) has armour 4, not 5.
18. **Numbers, genres and groups (2026-09-26).** Every playable breed has a fixed **number** in the master chart (Rules table 16.8, the Species booklet, the wiki), which holds all its details. Breeds are also sorted into **genres** (classic fantasy, fairy tale and folklore, myth and legend, gothic horror, space opera, robots and synthetics, super-powered and so on, 22 in all, under three kinds of world: fantasy and myth, science fiction, super-powered), and each genre into **groups** of about 45 or fewer (elves; dwarves and small folk; cats, dogs, bears and weasels; and so on). The choosing lists give only numbers and names, and a breed can appear in more than one. **Genres are for finding, not for fencing:** they show where to look, and any breed the Game Master allows can join any game (talking animals turn up in the most classic fantasy). A Game Master may still name the genres to start from, or give a short list of numbers for their world. Ordinary humans (numbers 1 to 3) fit every genre. A human breed whose numbers are exactly those of a baseline human is not a separate choice: it is a look or a culture, played as a baseline human (with the matching culture, if it was one). About one breed in twenty is a **starter**: a plain, iconic choice with a one-line description, listed first.

**Balance count used below:** each Mod point counts 1 (+ or −); a Set counts its value; a Set to Foreign Concept counts −2; power affinity counts 0.
